The Mythology of the Mising

The mythology of the Mising people has survived across generations through an unbroken oral tradition. The Mibu, revered as the ritual specialists and spiritual custodians of the community, have preserved these sacred narratives through ceremonial chants known as Mibu A:bang—songs that carry the memory of creation, ancestry, and the cosmos itself.

Nothingness

Kéyum

There was neither light, nor matter, nor sound within the vastness of existence.

The First Unfolding

Yumkang

Within the eternal vacuum, a spontaneous vibration emerged. From its ceaseless movement arose Yumkang, the first unfolding of existence.

The Great Vibration

Ka:si

Ka:si, the Great Vibration, marked the awakening of creation itself.

Siang
A:bo
Bomuk
The Celestial Presence

Mukseng

Within the heart of the Great Vibration, a minute celestial presence emerged—appearing simultaneously in both seen and unseen forms.

Sedí Melo
Abu Sedí, the Creator

From the sacred union of Sedí and Melo emerged the foundations of existence itself. Abu Sedí, the Creator, transcends all creation and therefore requires no sacrifice.

The Great Mother

Pédong

Pédong, the Great Mother, whose name signifies "Rain," became the source of countless living beings and spirits—both benevolent and malevolent.

Ané Do:nyi Oddoké Abu Po:lo

Do:nyi · Po:lo

Mother Sun and Father Moon are believed to be the eyes of Sedí. Through them, Abu Sedí continues to watch over all existence.
