Kepang Érang

The Kepang Érang Members consist of four major clans: Pértín, Pérme, Borang, and Ra:tan. According to Mising oral traditions and historical accounts, these clans once lived in Damro Village in Arunachal Pradesh, alongside the Longgíngs. While the Longgíngs later descended from the hills into the plains, the Kepang clans remained in the region for a longer period before becoming part of the Mising tribal fold.

Compared to many other established Mising clans, the Kepangs are considered to have joined the Mising community relatively recently. They are believed to have descended from the Padam people, which helps explain why the clan names Pértín, Pérme, Borang, and Ra:tan can still be found among the Adi communities today. This shared presence is seen as a reflection of their historical connections and relatively recent migration and integration into the Mising community.

The Kepang Érang is also mentioned in the Mising folktale "Why the Longgíngs Left the Hills," which recounts the relationship and conflict between the Kepang and Longgíng clans and provides a traditional account of the Longgíng clan members' departure from the hills.

  • Pértín is commonly written as Pertin.
  • Pérme is commonly written as Permey and Perme.
  • Borang is generally written as Borang.
  • Ra:tan is commonly written as Ratan.
