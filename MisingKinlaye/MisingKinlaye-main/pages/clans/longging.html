Longgíng Érang

Longgíng was an ancestral clan from which several Mising clans are believed to have descended, including Panggíng, Lagasu:, Noro, Pogag, and Sinte. Genealogically, the Longgíng Clan is believed to have belonged to the Minyong group.

A folktale known as "Why the Longgíngs Left the Hills" is associated with the Longgíng Clan. The story narrates the conflict between the Kepang Clan and the Longgíng Clan, explaining the reason behind the Longgíngs departure from the hills.

Over time, several clan names have developed different spellings within the Mising community:

  • Panggíng is commonly written as Panging.
  • Lagasu:/Lagasung is commonly written as Lagachu, Lagashu, and Lagasu.
  • Noro is commonly written as Narah among many Misings today.
  • Pogag is written as both Pogag and Pagag within the Mising community.
  • Sinte is commonly written as Chintey among the Mising people.

These variations reflect differences in pronunciation, regional usage, and the way Mising clan names have been recorded over time.
